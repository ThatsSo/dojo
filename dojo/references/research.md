# Research the target

Use this during Planning (coach mode) or Design when the learner is aiming at something defined by someone else. Guessing what an exam "probably" covers wastes the learner's limited practice time; the real outline usually exists and can be found.

**The concept:** this is *Ultralearning*'s metalearning step, specifically "benchmarking": before practising, find out how the target is actually measured and how others have prepared for it. It decides what goes on the skill map (S1) and what "practising in the real context" means (S2).

## When to research

Research is **required** when the goal names an external target:
- an exam, certification or licence (e.g. the CPA AUD section, a driving theory test);
- a grade or level defined by someone else (an ABRSM music grade, a CEFR language level, a belt test);
- a specific course or syllabus;
- a job skill with a published standard.

**Skip it** when there's no external target ("learn to juggle", "get better at mental math"). Then the skill map comes from the learner's own goal. Say in one line that no research is needed and why.

## What to find

Look for **official sources first** (the body that sets the exam or standard), then reputable secondary sources (well-known prep providers, recent candidate reports):

1. **Content outline and section weights:** what's covered, and what share of the score each area carries.
2. **Format:** question types (multiple choice, simulations, essays, practical), the number of questions, and the time per section.
3. **Scoring:** the scale, the pass mark, and how partial credit or unscored items work.
4. **Official sample or practice questions:** link them; they show the real difficulty and wording.
5. **Recent changes:** new blueprint versions, effective dates, and topics added or removed.
6. **Recommended materials** and how candidates usually prepare. Note typical study hours if they're published.

## How to use it

- **Sources:** cite every source with its URL and the date you checked it. Mark anything you inferred rather than found ("inferred from the outline's wording").
- **Build the skill map from the blueprint:** categories and subcategories follow the official outline, and practice time is weighted by section weight. Heavy sections get more sessions, but no section gets none.
- **Match the format in later stages:** Perform-stage practice and benchmarks use the real question types, timing and conditions (S2, S11).
- **Ask the learner only for what can't be looked up:** their exam date, current level, materials they already have, and time available per week.
- **Explain the reasoning** (SKILL.md, hard rule 8). For example: "Section 3 is 30–40% of the score, so it gets about a third of your sessions."

## Without web access

Say so plainly ("I can't browse in this tool"). Ask the learner to paste or upload the official outline and any sample questions. **Never present a guessed outline as the real one.** If the learner can't provide it, build a provisional map, label it *provisional*, and replace it when the outline arrives.

## Research section template (for `plan.md`)

```markdown
## Research
Target: <exam / standard, version, effective date>
Checked: <date>

| Area | Weight | Notes |
|---|---|---|

- Format: <question types, count, timing>
- Scoring: <scale, pass mark>
- Sample questions: <link>
- Recent changes: <what, when>
- Sources:
  - <title> — <URL> (official)
  - <title> — <URL>
- Inferred, not confirmed: <anything guessed>
```
