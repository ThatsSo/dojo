# Pitfalls

The one merged list of what goes wrong. It combines the pitfall tables from the source write-ups (chicken sexing, progressive overload, Voltaic, Woodpecker) with the misapplications in `principles.md`, deduplicated.
- **Audits and lens checks:** scan the "What you see" column against the app or plan.
- **Coaching:** check a stalled plan against it before adding effort.
- **Columns:** "Stage" is the arc stage where the pitfall usually appears; "Principles" points to the rule that corrects it (see `rules.md`).

| Pitfall | What you see | Correction | Stage | Principles | Sources |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Undifferentiated practice | One "practice" mode and one overall score; nobody can say which part is weak | Break the skill into measurable sub-skills and hybrids | Map | S1 | Voltaic, Ultralearning |
| No baseline | Claims of progress with nothing to compare against; difficulty picked by feel | Measure accuracy, speed, independence and consistency before choosing the difficulty | Map | S1, S15 | Progressive overload |
| Misaligned practice | Drill scores rise while the real target doesn't; time spent on what's easy to practise | Train the specific capability needed (specificity); check against the real task | Map, Review | S2 | Progressive overload, Ultralearning |
| Incomplete coverage | Heavy drilling of a favourite area or a single method; other categories never practised | Keep balanced fundamentals; combine methods (direct practice, conceptual work, repetition) | Map | S3, S17 | Voltaic, Woodpecker |
| Passive exposure | Reading, watching or tapping to reveal without an attempt; "it looks familiar" | Require an answer, prediction or classification first | Acquire, Automate | S4 | Chicken sexing, Ultralearning, Flow |
| Premature answer exposure | The answer is visible, or one click away, before the learner commits | Commit to a prediction before checking | Acquire | S4 | Chicken sexing |
| Feedback-free repetition | Many reps, no correction; the same mistakes repeat with growing confidence | Give fast, reliable feedback on each attempt | Acquire | S6 | Chicken sexing, Voltaic, Ultralearning |
| Mindless grinding (junk volume) | Long sessions with no goal per block; time logged instead of meaningful attempts | Give each block a specific aim; count deliberate attempts | Any | S3, A4 | Voltaic, progressive overload |
| Excessive difficulty | Failing most attempts; technique distorts to survive; reliance on shortcuts | Step back a rung, or learn the missing prerequisite | Acquire | S8 | Voltaic, progressive overload, Woodpecker |
| Permanent underload | Everything feels easy; boredom; no progress for weeks | Raise one meaningful variable as competence grows | Automate | S8, S15 | Progressive overload, Flow |
| Increasing everything at once | A level-up changes several variables together; accuracy collapses | Raise one primary variable; ease the others | Acquire | S10 | Progressive overload, Voltaic, James |
| Progressing too quickly | Advancing after one lucky success | Require consistent competence (cleared rungs, repeated success) | Acquire | S13, S15 | Progressive overload, Voltaic |
| Rigid progression | Advancing on the calendar, or never advancing, regardless of results | Progress on performance, not time | Any | S13 | Progressive overload |
| Premature or superficial speed | Time pressure before accuracy; fast wrong answers; speed rises while comprehension falls | Accuracy first; compress time only while accuracy holds | Acquire, Automate | S9 | Chicken sexing, progressive overload, Woodpecker, James |
| Answer memorization (overfitting) | Practised items answered instantly, new ones failed; "remembers the answer" without the reason | Vary the set, require the why, test unfamiliar items | Automate | S12 | Woodpecker, Voltaic, chicken sexing |
| False pattern learning | The learner keys on an irrelevant cue (position, formatting, order) | Vary superficial features; teach the diagnostic cue | Acquire | S12, S14 | Chicken sexing |
| Poor transfer (false progress) | Drill gains don't show up in real performance or on unfamiliar problems | Test on real tasks and new material; increase direct practice | Review | S2 | Voltaic, Woodpecker, chicken sexing, progressive overload |
| Assistance dependence | Succeeds only with hints, worked examples or keys lit | Fade the scaffolding step by step | Acquire | S15, S16 | Progressive overload |
| Unnecessary complexity | Confusing instructions, clutter, theory dumps, needless steps | Minimize extraneous load; give theory in small doses | Acquire | S5 | Progressive overload, James |
| Over-reliance on explanation | Can explain the rule but can't recognize or perform it at speed | Add discrimination practice and volume with feedback | Acquire, Automate | S14 | Chicken sexing, Zen in the Art of Archery |
| Over-reliance on intuition | Confident snap judgements that measurements don't support; gut feel in noisy domains | Measure accuracy; add explicit cues; trust intuition only where patterns are stable and feedback is reliable | Review | S14, S11 | Chicken sexing |
| Recognition mistaken for understanding | Picks the right option but can't explain or derive it, or the reverse | Train and test recognition and explanation separately | Review | S5, S4 | Chicken sexing, Ultralearning |
| Ignored persistent errors | The same items fail cycle after cycle | Isolate them, find the misunderstood distinction, drill them separately | Any | S3, S6 | Woodpecker, chicken sexing |
| Training on the test (cheesing) | Scores rise through exploits, settings tricks or memorized benchmark items; faked technique | Separate assessment from training; require authentic execution | Review | S11 | Voltaic, Zen in the Art of Archery |
| Peak-score obsession | The best run treated as the real level; bad days hidden | Track averages, the worst performance and the spread | Review | A6 | Voltaic |
| Plateau answered with effort | More volume, same results, growing frustration | Audit fundamentals (technique, setup, fatigue, design) and adapt the method before adding volume | Review | S17, S8 | Voltaic, Zen in the Art of Archery |
| Overtraining and burnout | Fatigue, falling accuracy, dread, skipped sessions | Cut the load, deload, protect sleep and breaks | Any | A4, S15 | Progressive overload, Voltaic, Woodpecker |
| Performance overthinking | Conscious correction mid-performance; choking; flow breaks | Separate learning and performance zones; trust trained technique | Perform | A3, A5, S14 | Voltaic, Zen in the Art of Archery |
| Forcing and tension | Strain, rushing, gripping, breath held | Relax, breathe, remove unnecessary effort | Perform | A5 | Zen in the Art of Archery, Voltaic |
| Judging mid-task | Scolding or self-praise during practice; red-X and buzzer feedback; streaks that punish | Observe, don't judge; evaluate afterwards; process-first metrics | Any | A1, A3 | Zen in the Art of Archery, Flow, James |
| Treating a method as proven | Following a protocol rigidly though it isn't working; overstating its evidence | Treat the protocol as an experiment; adapt it to results | Review | S17 | Woodpecker, Ultralearning |
| Leaking attention | Notifications, multitasking, a cluttered session start | Protect attention; use a short ritual to arrive present | Any | A5 | Flow, Ultralearning, Zen in the Art of Archery |
| Isolated skills | Skills taught as unconnected tricks; no stated purpose; practice feels like a chore | Link new material to known material and to the learner's purpose | Map | A8 | Flow, Zen in the Art of Archery |
