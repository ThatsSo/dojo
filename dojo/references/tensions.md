# Tensions and their resolutions

The sources look like they disagree in several places. Almost every case is two stages of the arc being confused (see `principles.md`), or structure being confused with attitude. When designing or auditing, check the work against each row. A design that picks one side of a tension without saying which stage it serves is usually wrong for the other stage.

| ID | Tension | Resolution | In app design |
|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | **Speed vs. accuracy:** push the pace (*Ultralearning*'s intensity, Woodpecker's halving time) or slow down (James, Voltaic)? | **Accuracy first, then speed.** All the sources agree once stages are separated: get near-perfect accuracy (Acquire), then compress time (Automate). Speed goes up only while accuracy holds. | Tempo and time limits rise only after clean runs; they drop automatically when accuracy falls; accuracy and speed are both shown. (S8, S9) |
| T2 | **Stop or continue on a mistake:** modern practice says catch and correct immediately; Zen and flow say don't let a mistake break the state. | **It depends on the zone.** Learning zone: pause, rewind, correct. Performance zone: notice, log, keep going. Logged mistakes become later drills. | Separate drill and run-through modes, with a mistake log that feeds the drills (e.g., Piaano's Wait vs. Scroll/Follow Me). (A3, S6) |
| T3 | **Goals vs. detachment:** accelerated learning is built on goals; Zen asks you to drop attachment to them. | **A rigorous plan, done with a Zen attitude.** Goals shape the plan; the session is judged by whether practice happened. You're in charge of the goals; they aren't in charge of you. | Goals the learner sets and can edit; practice-done as the main daily metric; no guilt mechanics. (A1, S1) |
| T4 | **Intensity vs. no timeframes:** *Ultralearning* is aggressive and time-boxed; James says don't set a time frame. | **Schedule the inputs, not the outcomes.** Fix when and how much you practise; move on when the habit has formed, not by a date. | Practice schedules and reminders, but unlocks based on skill, never on the calendar. (S13, A2) |
| T5 | **Repetition vs. variety:** Woodpecker repeats one fixed set; Voltaic alternates scenarios to avoid pattern recognition. | **The repetition-target rule.** If the patterns are the skill, repeat the same set. If the set stands in for a general skill, vary it. | Fixed sets for chords, motifs and vocabulary; generated or rotating material for sight-reading, aim and comprehension. (S12) |
| T6 | **React vs. predict:** reacting with your eyes is too late; predicting builds bad habits against unpredictable input. | **"Feeling":** let the trained body respond before conscious perception gets involved. Train reaction against unpredictable input until it becomes implicit; prediction is a later, real-world layer. | Unpredictable drill material for reactive skills; avoid patterns the learner can game. (A5, S14) |
| T7 | **Explicit vs. implicit:** explain everything, or learn only by doing? | **It depends on how fast decisions must be made.** Explicit for slow, complex decisions; implicit for fast execution. Small, well-chosen explicit cues speed up implicit learning. | Theory and strategy separate from execution drills; brief cues, not lectures, inside drills. (S14, S5) |
| T8 | **Rigour vs. enjoyment:** grind the weakness hard, or keep the work enjoyable? | **Dosed intensity.** Grinding only works for people who still enjoy it. Use short bottleneck blocks inside varied, enjoyable all-round practice. | Short, capped drill blocks; variety; rest prompts; bottleneck add-ons on top of a balanced default. (A4, S3) |
| T9 | **Flow as the ideal vs. flow often unavailable.** | **Slow conscious effort still counts.** Set up the conditions for flow, but never make a session's value depend on reaching it. Don't let the perfect be the enemy of the good. | "Easy day" or low-energy options that still count; no messaging that treats a non-flow session as a failure. (A7, A5) |
| T10 | **Measuring vs. not clinging:** benchmarks and data, or mastery as something internal? | **Benchmarks are information, not self-worth.** Measure rarely, consistently and honestly; judge by consistency; don't make the score the point. | A separate, infrequent benchmark mode; averages and ranges; neutral presentation; no leaderboard as the main motivator. (S11, A6) |
