# Dojo rules

The surface layer: read this first, in every mode. It is the one binding list of rules, merged from James's synthesis and every source write-up.
- **Each principle** (bold, with its ID) is a one-line rule with the concept behind it.
- **The bullets under it** are operational rules that apply it, with the sources they came from.
- **Drill down when you need to:** `principles.md` explains why a rule holds, how to spot it, its misapplications and its evidence level. `pitfalls.md` lists what goes wrong. `sources/` holds the evidence.

Before applying a rule, name the arc stage you're in (Map, Acquire, Automate, Perform, Review); many rules belong to one stage only. Conflicts between rules are resolved in `tensions.md`.

**Thesis:** a rigorous plan, done with a Zen attitude. Structure decides what, when and at what difficulty; attitude decides how to be while practising.

## Structure

**S1 Map and decompose.** Break the skill into categories and subcategories that each isolate one technique, plus hybrids that recombine them, because one overall score hides which part is weak.
- Define the target capability or perceptual task precisely before choosing drills. *(Voltaic, chicken sexing, progressive overload)*
- Establish a baseline first: accuracy, speed, complexity, independence, consistency. *(progressive overload)*
- Isolate components to diagnose them, then train hybrids that recombine them. *(Voltaic)*
- Spend roughly the first tenth of the effort researching how the skill is learned and measured (`research.md` for exams). *(Ultralearning)*

**S2 Practise the real thing; check transfer.** Practise in the real context, and treat gains in drills as unproven until they show up in real performance.
- Train what you want to improve (specificity): match the overload to the capability you need. *(progressive overload)*
- Test on unfamiliar examples, without hints, before calling a skill learned. *(chicken sexing, Woodpecker, Voltaic, progressive overload)*
- Validate on complete real tasks, not only isolated drills. *(Voltaic, Woodpecker, chicken sexing)*
- Prefer performing the skill over consuming material about it. *(Flow, Ultralearning)*

**S3 All-round practice, plus the bottleneck.** Cover every category at the learner's level, then add short targeted work on the weakest part and fold it back in.
- Keep balanced fundamentals; add specialized drills only once the foundation exists. *(Voltaic)*
- Use broad routines for general growth and focused sessions for specific deficits; rotating one focus per session is a valid alternative. *(Voltaic)*
- Isolate persistently failed items and work on them separately. *(Woodpecker, chicken sexing)*
- Interleave topics in review so the learner has to identify what each item is asking. *(spaced repetition)*

**S4 Retrieve and generate.** Producing an answer from memory teaches far more than rereading it, so the learner commits to an answer before seeing it.
- Require an independent prediction or classification before the answer is shown. *(chicken sexing)*
- Test instead of reviewing; write, teach and explain back. *(Ultralearning, spaced repetition, James)*

**S5 Understand before memorizing; manage load.** Build understanding first, then use memory tools to keep it; add relevant difficulty without unnecessary complexity.
- Minimize extraneous load: unclear instructions, distractions, needless steps. *(progressive overload)*
- Train and test recognition and explanation separately. Being able to recognize the right answer isn't understanding why it's right, and the reverse holds too. *(chicken sexing, Ultralearning)*
- Solve, derive and explain rather than merely recognize. *(Ultralearning)*

**S6 Fast, informative, non-punitive feedback.** Feedback should arrive quickly and say what to fix; repetition without feedback can strengthen errors.
- Check every decision against a reliable answer; feedback is only as good as its labels. *(chicken sexing)*
- Examine persistent errors to find the misunderstood distinction, not just repeat the item. *(chicken sexing, Woodpecker)*
- Record and review performance (recordings, logs) to catch errors you can't see in the moment; ask outside eyes. *(Voltaic, James)*
- Grade honestly; illusions of competence come from familiarity. *(spaced repetition)*
- Practise deliberately: observe, diagnose, target, execute, evaluate. *(Voltaic)*

**S7 Space for retention; repeat for automaticity.** Spread reviews out with growing intervals so knowledge lasts, and repeat skills past first success until they're automatic.
- Extend retrieval intervals as retention stabilizes. *(progressive overload, spaced repetition)*
- Overlearn: keep practising beyond first success until it's effortless. *(Woodpecker)*
- A small daily habit compounds; trust the schedule. *(spaced repetition)*
- Cap new cards per day (new cards create reviews for weeks) and set a target retention (about 85–90%) rather than maximizing it. *(spaced repetition)*

**S8 Stay in the goldilocks zone.** Keep difficulty just above current skill; too easy bores, and too hard teaches the wrong technique.
- Lower difficulty when overwhelmed; raise it when bored. *(Flow)*
- Use productive overload, not underload or excessive overload; individualize to actual results. *(progressive overload)*
- Reassess as yesterday's hard task becomes routine. *(progressive overload)*
- Order a session from preparation to demanding work. *(Voltaic)*

**S9 Accuracy first, then speed.** Speed rises only while accuracy holds, because repeated errors become habits.
- Build reliable judgments before demanding fast ones. *(chicken sexing, Woodpecker, progressive overload)*
- Never trade genuine understanding for superficial speed. *(Woodpecker)*
- Automate the fundamentals before advancing. *(Zen in the Art of Archery)*

**S10 One variable at a time, within a difficulty budget.** Add complexity one variable at a time; when one goes up, ease the others.
- Establish fundamentals before adding variables. *(Voltaic)*
- Combine already-mastered parts into more demanding tasks (progressive complexity). *(progressive overload)*

**S11 Measure separately from training.** Benchmarks measure; drills improve. Never train on the test or exploit the task.
- Use multiple measures: accuracy, speed, consistency, generalization. *(chicken sexing, Voltaic)*
- Never optimize a score by bypassing the skill ("cheesing"), and reject shortcuts that only simulate competence. *(Voltaic, Zen in the Art of Archery)*
- Measure intuition, don't trust it: confidence isn't accuracy. *(chicken sexing)*
- Track time and errors, including recurring errors, not just scores. *(Woodpecker, chicken sexing)*

**S12 The repetition-target rule.** If the patterns *are* the skill, repeat the same set; if the set stands in for a general skill, vary it so the learner doesn't memorize the material.
- Use large, varied, representative example sets. *(chicken sexing, Woodpecker)*
- Alternate similar drills to prevent scenario memorization. *(Voltaic)*
- Learn the principles connecting examples, not their specific answers. *(Woodpecker)*

**S13 Mastery-based progression.** Schedule the inputs; advance on demonstrated, consistent competence, never on the calendar or one lucky success.
- Earn progression by consistent performance, not a single success. *(progressive overload, Voltaic)*

**S14 Explicit and implicit, by decision speed.** Explicit knowledge for slow, complex decisions; implicit skill for fast execution. Perceptual skill grows from volume plus feedback, sped up by a few explicit cues.
- Teach the known diagnostic features first, then build recognition through examples. *(chicken sexing)*
- Repeat at high volume, with feedback, for recognition to develop. *(chicken sexing)*
- Let practical experience come before complete intellectual understanding. *(Zen in the Art of Archery)*
- Trust trained intuition only where patterns are stable and feedback is accurate. *(chicken sexing)*
- Practise consciously, then trust trained technique in performance. *(Zen in the Art of Archery)*

**S15 Progressive overload.** Raise load in small, tracked steps, one variable at a time, and plan easier recovery periods.
- Use the smallest meaningful increment (microloading). *(progressive overload)*
- Overload variables: intensity, volume, density, complexity, precision, independence, variability, retention. *(progressive overload)*
- Fade scaffolding gradually: worked example → partial → hints → none → unfamiliar. *(progressive overload)*
- Count meaningful attempts, not study time. *(progressive overload)*
- Periodize: phases for foundation, volume, intensity, performance and assessment. *(progressive overload)*
- Deload when fatigue builds or performance deteriorates. *(progressive overload)*
- Compress time progressively on fixed sets, only while accuracy holds. *(Woodpecker)*
- Use harder-than-normal conditions only when technique and transfer stay intact. *(Voltaic)*

**S16 Models and worked examples.** Learn from demonstrations, worked solutions and expert-labelled cases, then fade the support.
- Observe and imitate skilled performers to pick up tacit knowledge. *(Zen in the Art of Archery)*
- Expose the learner to many realistic examples of each distinction. *(chicken sexing)*

**S17 Adapt the method to the evidence.** The plan is a hypothesis: watch whether the method works and change it when results stall.
- Revise methods by observed results rather than following an ineffective plan rigidly. *(Ultralearning)*
- Investigate plateaus (technique, setup, fatigue, design) before adding volume. *(Voltaic)*
- Treat specific protocols as experiments; supplement repetition with conceptual and applied work. *(Woodpecker)*

## Attitude

**A1 Process over progress.** Practising is the success; goals are ones you're in charge of, judged by execution quality rather than outcomes alone.
- Judge practice by the quality of execution. *(Zen in the Art of Archery)*
- Cultivate intrinsic motivation: satisfaction in challenge, curiosity and mastery. *(Flow)*
- Build internal engagement: use curiosity and mental challenges to make unstructured time rewarding. *(Flow)*

**A2 Discipline over motivation.** Build systems and small default actions that survive moods.
- Gamify repetition: goals, records, constraints and challenges inside routine practice. *(Flow)*

**A3 Observe, don't judge.** Feedback is information; in the learning zone fix mistakes, and in the performance zone notice and log them.
- Separate learning (analyze mechanics) from performance (let execution run). *(Voltaic)*
- Focus on execution during practice and evaluate afterward. *(Flow)*
- Reframe setbacks as new goals. *(Flow)*

**A4 Dosed intensity.** Rigour only works if enjoyment survives it: cap volume, take breaks, and recover.
- Keep volume sustainable and attentive; include breaks, sleep and recovery. *(Voltaic, progressive overload, Woodpecker)*
- Balance intensity and volume without sacrificing quality. *(progressive overload)*
- Embrace difficult practice when it improves learning, in doses you can sustain. *(Ultralearning)*

**A5 Focus plus relaxation.** Peak performance is relaxed, complete focus; set up the conditions for flow, but never force it.
- Design flow conditions: clear goals, immediate feedback, and challenge balanced to skill. *(Flow)*
- Define each session's objective before starting. *(Flow)*
- Protect attention: remove distractions. *(Flow, Ultralearning)*
- Reduce unnecessary tension and force; use breathing. *(Zen in the Art of Archery, Voltaic)*
- Use a short ritual to arrive present, so repetitions are mindful rather than mindless. *(Zen in the Art of Archery)*

**A6 Consistency as the measure.** Raise the floor and narrow the spread; mastery is internal, and assessments are information, not self-worth.
- Track averages, worst performances and consistency, not peaks. *(Voltaic)*

**A7 Peace of mind.** Know the direction is right even when some steps go backwards; slow conscious effort still counts.
- Continue attentive practice through plateaus and delayed improvement. *(Zen in the Art of Archery)*

**A8 Purpose and integration.** Connect learning to existing knowledge and a self-chosen purpose; mastery shapes the person too.
- Differentiate and integrate: build specialized skills, then connect them to what you know and to larger goals. *(Flow)*
- Organize learning around a self-directed life theme. *(Flow)*
- Use mastery training to develop patience, composure and humility. *(Zen in the Art of Archery)*
